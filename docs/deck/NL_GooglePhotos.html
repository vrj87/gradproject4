Google Photos · Core Experience

The job is finding a photo you still picture — not a better search box

People start with a fragment of memory. Success is opening the photo they meant. Faster indexing or a smarter keyword rank is a different product.

North-star metric

Successful vague-memory retrieval rate = users who find the intended photo on a vague-memory task ÷ users who start a search with incomplete memory.

Out of scope

Search latency, ranking of exact keywords, and "make search feel faster."

Problem definition (Phase 4)

People remember how a place felt on a trip and have forgotten the date, album, and town name

Target segment

S2 Trip/event rememberer. Large library. Remembers trip by vibe (café, lights, boat). Forgotten date, album, town name.

Retrieval scenario

The café I cannot date — a trip photo the person can still picture when capture day and place name are gone.

Product outcome

Understand — the product accepts vibe, object, episode, and fuzzy time. Exactly one node.

Root cause: Search cannot take the clue they still have even though the photo is in the library. Workarounds: scroll, ask a person, another app, still unfound. Business value: library people cannot re-enter becomes write-only backup.

Locked: proceed · S2 · understand · n=6 7 / 10

Solution & MVP (Phase 5)

Remember puts intelligence at the moment of understanding: a vibe in, a trip group out

1Offer a fragment

3Confirm or steer

Open the photo, or say "not this trip."

Success metrics & risks (Phases 7-8)

We will know it worked when more of these searches end on the right photo

Leading metrics
  • Clue submitted as memory language (without date first)
  • MemoryQuery holds the locked cue type (placeVibe, object, episode, fuzzy time)
  • Target in the first grouped set
  • Target opened from a group
  • Success after refine
Risks & mitigations
  • Read as "better search" → composer asks for fragment, not "Search your photos"
  • Model invents date/town → ground MemoryQuery in typed tokens; strip assistantText matching date/GPS patterns
  • Learned café task success → use respondent's Phase 3 task when original photo can be used

Guardrails: control date task still succeeds in Search; hallucinated EXIF rate stays at zero. Metrics are prototype signals, not Google Photos production telemetry.

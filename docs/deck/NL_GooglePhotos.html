The problem

People remember how a place felt on a trip and have forgotten the date, album, and town name

Target segment

Trip rememberers with large photo libraries. They recall a trip by its atmosphere, activity, or objects but have forgotten the date, album, or town.

Retrieval scenario

The café I cannot date — a trip photo the person can still picture when capture day and place name are gone.

Product outcome

Understand — the product accepts vibe, object, episode, and fuzzy time. Exactly one node.

Root cause: Search cannot use the clue that remains, even though the photo is in the library. People scroll, ask someone, try another app, or give up. Business risk: a library people cannot re-enter becomes a write-only backup.

Decision: proceed · audience: trip rememberers · focus: understanding · six respondents 7 / 10

Success metrics & risks

We will know it worked when more of these searches end on the right photo

Leading metrics
  • Clue submitted as memory language (without date first)
  • Remember accepts place, object, activity, and approximate-time clues
  • Intended photo appears in the first grouped set
  • Intended photo opened from a group
  • Successful find after refining
Risks & mitigations
  • Read as "better search" → composer asks for fragment, not "Search your photos"
  • Model invents a date or town → ground interpretation in the user’s words and reject invented dates or location metadata
  • Learned café task success → use a respondent's own task when the original photo can be used

Guardrails: known-date searches should still succeed in Search; Remember must not invent capture dates or location metadata. Metrics are prototype signals, not Google Photos product metrics.
